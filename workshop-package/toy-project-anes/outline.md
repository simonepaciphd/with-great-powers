# Political efficacy and turnout — project outline

## Research question

Does political efficacy shape turnout independently of partisan strength, or is its effect mediated through partisan intensity?

## Why it matters

Efficacy — the belief that one can understand and influence politics (internal) and that government responds to people like you (external) — is a load-bearing construct in the civic-engagement literature. If efficacy's effect on turnout operates through partisan identity, then the policy implication is different than if efficacy matters in its own right: the former suggests mobilization strategies rooted in group attachment; the latter suggests information and procedural interventions.

## Data

ANES 2024 Time Series Study (`data/anes_timeseries_2024_csv_YYYYMMDD.csv` — download per `data/DOWNLOAD.md`). The codebook in `data/` documents all efficacy items across the pre- and post-election waves.

## The measurement problem

Political efficacy is a latent construct with two standard dimensions (internal vs. external). ANES has carried efficacy items in various wordings across decades; different wordings emphasize different aspects. The workshop exercise is to pick the operational definition you will defend, decide whether to treat the two dimensions as one index or as separate variables, and articulate the validity threats (social desirability bias, question order, acquiescence).
