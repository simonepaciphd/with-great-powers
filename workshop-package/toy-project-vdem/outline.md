# Clientelism across democratic regimes — project outline

## Research question

How does clientelism vary across nominally democratic regimes, and what does that variation imply about the function of elections in those systems?

## Why it matters

Clientelism — the conditional exchange of particularistic benefits for political support — is widely diagnosed but hard to measure. Most existing scholarship either relies on single-country fieldwork or treats the concept as a dichotomous regime type. A cross-national graded measure, if defensible, would let us ask whether clientelism and electoral competition coexist on the same continuum or represent orthogonal dimensions of democratic practice.

## Data

V-Dem v16 (`data/V-Dem-CY-FullOthers-v16_csv.zip`). V-Dem publishes a clientelism index (`v2xnp_client`) built from several expert-coded component items; the codebook inside the zip documents the aggregation.

## The measurement problem

"Clientelism" is a latent construct. V-Dem's composite score is one defensible operationalization, but the underlying items capture different slices of the concept (vote buying, programmatic vs. particularistic linkages, sanctioning of non-compliant voters). The workshop exercise is to decide which indicators belong in *your* operational definition, justify that choice, and flag validity threats.
