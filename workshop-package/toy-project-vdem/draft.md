# Clientelism across democratic regimes — draft

## Introduction (partial)

Observers of electoral politics in the Global South have long noted the persistence of clientelist exchange even in formally competitive elections. Parties distribute jobs, goods, and favors to voters who, in turn, deliver their support at the ballot box. The question this paper asks is not whether clientelism exists, but how much it varies across regimes that look democratic on paper, and whether that variation tracks other features of the electoral process — competitiveness, turnout, incumbency advantage — in ways that illuminate what elections are *for* in each case.
