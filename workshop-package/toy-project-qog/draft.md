# Dimensions of state capacity — draft

## Introduction (partial)

Comparative scholars routinely invoke "state capacity" as an explanans for an astonishing range of outcomes — from economic growth to human rights protection to peace agreement durability. Yet the concept is rarely pinned to a specific operational definition, and the cross-national indicators researchers reach for (WGI government effectiveness, ICRG bureaucracy quality, BTI state capacity, rule of law) measure conceptually adjacent but empirically distinct things. This paper treats that messiness as the object of inquiry rather than a nuisance: which dimension of state capacity, measured how, actually predicts the outcome we care about, and how much does the choice matter?
