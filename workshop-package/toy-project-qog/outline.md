# Dimensions of state capacity — project outline

## Research question

Which dimension of state capacity — administrative, extractive, or coercive — best predicts variation in service delivery quality across countries?

## Why it matters

"State capacity" appears in a wide range of arguments — development, conflict, democratic consolidation — but the concept is multidimensional and the available cross-national indicators measure overlapping but distinct things (WGI government effectiveness, ICRG bureaucracy quality, BTI state capacity, rule of law). If the dimensions behave differently with respect to outcomes of interest, treating them as interchangeable is a measurement error with substantive consequences.

## Data

QoG Standard Dataset, cross-section, January 2026 release (`data/qog_std_cs_jan26.csv`). Contains one row per country with hundreds of columns drawn from WGI, ICRG, BTI, V-Dem, Freedom House, and others. No codebook PDF shipped in the zip; the QoG website publishes the full codebook online.

## The measurement problem

"State capacity" is a latent construct with at least three contested dimensions. Each dimension has multiple candidate indicators. The workshop exercise is to pick the dimension(s) and indicator(s) you will use, defend the choice on conceptual and empirical grounds, and flag the validity threats (each of the big cross-national indices has its own measurement critique).
