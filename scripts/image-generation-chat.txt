Thought for 9s

I read the site closely. The strongest visual logic is not just “bad AI use vs good AI use,” but black-box delegation vs researcher-controlled, transparent orchestration. Your page is very explicit that the contrast turns on two governing principles—researcher control and radical transparency—and on the idea that AI should be embedded in a structured workflow rather than treated as an all-purpose oracle.

A few things from the site feel especially important for the infographic concept:

The “good” side should not look like the researcher manually doing everything. It should look like delegation inside a governed system: repo, agent rules, skills, prompts, verification, logs, provenance flags, and human control over substantive decisions. That is the core of your argument.

The “bad” side should not be caricatured as simply “using chat.” The sharper contrast is: the researcher offloads substantive judgment to a chat model, asks for a DID to prove a desired result, receives outputs that flow straight into the paper, and leaves no transparent record of process, provenance, or verification. Your site frames this as exactly the kind of practice that risks a wider replicability gap.

The “good” side should probably visually incorporate at least some elements from your setup stack: tool/harness, context repository, prompt structure, agent management, plus the transparency layer of asset registry, interaction log, provenance, verification. Those are distinctive and make the image yours rather than generic AI-governance art.

The DID example is a smart choice, because your site explicitly includes empirics as a stage where agents can help implement pipelines, run specification sweeps, and produce tables/figures—while the researcher still retains control over design and interpretation.
