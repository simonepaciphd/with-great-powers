# Adoption & Heterogeneity — Statistics for §1.1

## Researcher-specific adoption

### Wiley 2025 researcher survey (most cited figure)
- **84%** of researchers report using AI tools (survey of 2,430 researchers, August 2025).
- Of those, **80%** use general-purpose tools (ChatGPT-style); only **25%** use purpose-built AI research assistants.
- Source: https://newsroom.wiley.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2025/AI-Adoption-Jumps-to-84-Among-Researchers-as-Expectations-Undergo-Significant-Reality-Check/default.aspx
- Primary report to be located before citing.

### Heterogeneity signals
- Wiley: adoption (84%) vs. use of *research-grade* tools (25%) reveals a **literacy gap** — most researchers are using AI, but the majority are using consumer chat interfaces, not workflows that unlock agentic capabilities. This directly motivates the paper's "basic AI literacy" contribution.
- Pepinsky (*Journal of East Asian Studies* editor): "at least a half-dozen" LLM-written papers observed in the pipeline — uneven adoption visible at the referee/editor level.
- Kevin Munger prediction: **50% increase** in submissions to top political-science journals (cited via Brookings — trace to primary source).

## General population adoption (context, not researcher-specific)

### Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index
- GenAI reached **53% population adoption within three years** — faster than PC or internet.
- US: 28.3% population adoption (ranks 24th globally).
- AI-related publications in natural/physical/life sciences grew **26–28% YoY**.
- Source: https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report

### US adoption — diverging estimates (illustrates heterogeneity of measurement)
- Stanford HAI: **28.3%** (US population).
- St. Louis Fed tracker: **54%** (August 2025).
- Federal Reserve feds-notes: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html
- Useful framing: adoption numbers are methodologically fragile — itself part of the story.

## Suggested use in §1.1

Three numbers that together tell the "revolution, unevenly distributed" story:

1. **53% gen-AI population adoption in 3 years (HAI 2026)** → revolution framing.
2. **84% researcher adoption / 25% research-tool adoption (Wiley 2025)** → uptake is near-universal at the surface, but the depth is thin — literacy gap.
3. **26–28% YoY growth in AI-related science publications (HAI 2026)** → the research frontier is moving fast.
