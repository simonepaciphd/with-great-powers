## Why this matters for our paper

Pepinsky is writing on exactly our topic, from the same principled angle (control / skepticism of interpretation), and is a credible political-science voice. This is both an ally and a risk — we need to differentiate our contribution.

## His thesis

Agentic AI (specifically Claude Code) is genuinely transformative for **rule-following tasks** in social science (data cleaning, code translation, specification sweeps) but **unreliable for interpretation, prose, citations, or substantive analysis**.

Core rule: *"Employ agentic AI for tasks that involve following rules. Do not use agentic AI for tasks that generate answers, arguments, or interpretations."*

## Concrete applications he names

- Loading CSV survey data → regressions + visualizations
- Data cleaning + format conversion (Stata ↔ R)
- Heterogeneous treatment effect tests across demographic subgroups
- Robustness-check sweeps with different specifications
- Publication-ready tables and figures

## Heterogeneity observation

At *Journal of East Asian Studies*, "at least a half-dozen papers written by generative AI" have appeared, with more expected. Signals uneven adoption and uneven detection capacity across referees/editors.

## His core concern

"P-hacking of the worst form": Claude Code may subtly produce results that match researcher expectations, sometimes offering unsolicited interpretations that nudge the researcher toward a particular result. The risk is not malice but **unconscious model selection** mediated by the tool.

## How our paper relates

**Alignment:** Both papers put researcher control at the center. Our framing of *"control"* as a governing principle is consistent with Pepinsky's "rules-only" rule.

**Differentiation:**
1. We go beyond a single rule to a **full workflow** (Figure 1: tool → harness → context → prompt → agent management).
2. We formalize **transparency mechanisms** (asset registry, interaction log, Data-NoMAD-style attestation) — Pepinsky raises the risk but doesn't prescribe infrastructure.
3. We recognize **agentic AI for more than rule-following**, with guardrails (validation, sign-off at key decision points) rather than a blanket interpretation ban. Worth engaging directly with Pepinsky's harder line and defending the calibrated position.
4. Our audience is broader (primer + best practices) vs. his commentary format.
