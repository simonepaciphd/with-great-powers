# Frontier Applications — Examples for §1.1

**Verification:** partially-verified (from search summaries; read primary sources before citing).

Goal: 3–5 concrete, recent (2025–2026) examples that show what agentic AI *unlocks* for social science research, not just what it assists. Each should be legible to a researcher skimming the intro.

---

## Candidate applications

### 1. LLM-agent research platforms (S-Researcher, human-AI collab)
*"LLM Agents as Social Scientists: A Human–AI Collaborative Platform for Social Science Automation"* (arXiv:2604.01520).
- Platforms that automate portions of the research process ("siliconizing" the participant pool and parts of the workflow).
- Useful as an example of *orchestrated* agentic systems targeted at social science.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01520

### 2. Large-scale LLM-driven social simulation (AgentSociety)
*"AgentSociety: Large-Scale Simulation of LLM-Driven Generative Agents"* (arXiv:2502.08691).
- Simulated societies of LLM agents grounded in psychology, economics, behavioral-science theory.
- Use cases: polarization, misinformation spread, policy-impact exploration.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08691v1

### 3. Emergent social conventions in LLM populations
*"Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations"* (Science Advances).
- Demonstrates spontaneous emergence of shared conventions in decentralized LLM agent populations.
- Useful as a "this is a whole new experimental paradigm" example.
- Source: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adu9368

### 4. Multi-agent LLM systems as a new social-science paradigm
*"Beyond Static Responses: Multi-Agent LLM Systems as a New Paradigm for Social Science Research"* (arXiv:2506.01839).
- Explicit argument that multi-agent systems are qualitatively different from single-agent LLM use in social science.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01839v2

### 5. Agentic AI for routine empirical work (Pepinsky examples)
- Data cleaning / format conversion (Stata ↔ R), regression pipelines with visualizations, HTE sweeps across demographic subgroups, specification-robustness sweeps, publication-ready tables + figures.
- Less a "frontier" than a "new baseline" — but arguably *this* is what's revolutionary for the median social scientist right now, not the simulation platforms.
- Citation: Pepinsky (2026), `pepinsky-agentic-ai.md`.

## Suggested use in §1.1

Pair a "frontier" example (AgentSociety or emergent-conventions) with a "new baseline" example (Pepinsky-style routine workflow) to capture both ends of the revolution the paper is describing — the exotic new possibilities *and* the quiet productivity shift.
