# cognitive-effects-on-researchers — Literature landscape

## Top 3 most relevant sources
1. `lee2025critical` — Best direct evidence on knowledge workers: it shows that higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical-thinking effort, while higher self-confidence predicts more critical engagement.
2. `shen2026skill` — Strongest skill-formation experiment in a professional-style setting: novice developers learning a new library lose conceptual understanding, code-reading, and debugging skill when they delegate too much to AI.
3. `fang2025criticalpaper` — Most directly researcher-specific source found: junior researchers using agent-mediated thought exchanges during paper reading improved critical-thinking scores over two weeks.

## Themes identified
- **Critical thinking often shifts rather than disappears**: In knowledge work, AI does not eliminate thinking so much as redirect it toward verification, integration, and stewardship, but often with lower perceived effort and weaker independent problem-solving. Representative sources: `lee2025critical`, `tankelevitch2024metacognitive`, `shihab2025copilot`.
- **Cognitive offloading and metacognitive weakening are the main mechanisms**: The strongest mechanism language is about reduced monitoring, control, planning, and evaluation rather than simple “laziness.” Representative sources: `tankelevitch2024metacognitive`, `gerlich2025offloading`, `prather2024widening`, `fan2025laziness`.
- **Unguided AI often improves immediate performance while harming learning**: Several studies show a performance–learning tradeoff, especially when users can copy, delegate, or bypass error-driven struggle. Representative sources: `bastani2025guardrails`, `shen2026skill`, `shihab2025copilot`.
- **Structured friction and guided use can reverse the pattern**: Guardrails, metacognitive prompts, and critique-oriented interfaces can preserve or even improve higher-order reasoning. Representative sources: `kazemitabaar2024engagement`, `tian2026aact`, `xin2026ailiteracy`, `fang2025criticalpaper`.
- **The cleanest adjacent transfer literatures are medical decision support and computing education**: These are the domains with the clearest evidence on oversight, automation bias, self-regulation, and skill formation under AI assistance. Representative sources: `gaube2021susceptibility`, `xin2026ailiteracy`, `margulieux2024selfregulation`, `prather2024widening`.

## Tensions / debates
- **Performance gains versus learning gains**: AI often boosts assisted-task performance while leaving unaided performance unchanged or worse. Sources on the risk side: `bastani2025guardrails`, `shen2026skill`, `fan2025laziness`. Sources showing protective designs can help: `kazemitabaar2024engagement`, `tian2026aact`, `xin2026ailiteracy`.
- **Over-reliance versus calibrated collaboration**: Some studies emphasize cognitive offloading and susceptibility to bad advice (`gerlich2025offloading`, `gaube2021susceptibility`), while others show that properly designed systems can promote critique instead of passive acceptance (`fang2025criticalpaper`, `tian2026aact`).
- **Evidence quality remains uneven**: The field mixes strong randomized and longitudinal designs with self-report and preprint evidence. Stronger causal pieces include `bastani2025guardrails` and `xin2026ailiteracy`; more provisional but still useful sources include `shen2026skill`, `fang2025criticalpaper`, `tian2026aact`, and `shihab2025copilot`.

## Gaps
Direct evidence on **researchers, scientists, or PhD students using LLMs for core research tasks over extended periods** is still very thin. I found one promising junior-researcher paper-reading study (`fang2025criticalpaper`), but not a mature literature on long-run deskilling in literature review, theory-building, coding, or empirical analysis among researchers specifically. There is also little on multi-month transfer, calibration of trust, and whether early-career researchers lose verification/debugging habits under agentic workflows. The strand should therefore explicitly say that it relies on **adjacent transfer literatures** for much of the empirical base.
