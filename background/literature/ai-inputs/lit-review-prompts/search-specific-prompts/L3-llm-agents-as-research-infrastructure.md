## Prompt context

### Paper context
- **Title:** With Great Powers: A Practical Guide to Agentic AI for Social Science Research
- **Thesis:** Effective agentic-AI adoption in social-science research rests on two principles — researcher control and radical transparency.

### Strand
- **Name:** llm-agents-as-research-infrastructure *(multi-agent simulations of social phenomena; agents as the epistemic object rather than the researcher's assistant)*

### Research questions *(DRAFT — researcher to confirm / edit)*
1. What are the most developed applications of multi-agent LLM simulations to social-science questions (polarization, collective action, market behavior, opinion dynamics, norm emergence) in 2024–2026?
2. What frameworks or platforms — AgentSociety, Stanford Smallville, Concordia, and others — have emerged as infrastructure for these studies? What is the state of their uptake and critique?
3. What methodological standards or critiques have been proposed for validating LLM-agent simulation results against real human behavior? Are there documented cases of validation success, failure, or inconclusive outcomes?
4. How does the multi-agent LLM simulation literature relate to classical agent-based modeling in economics and computational social science — a continuation, a break, or a parallel track with different epistemic claims?

### Anchor sources (do not re-discover — find adjacent work)
- AgentSociety (arXiv:2502.08691) — https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08691v1
- "Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations," *Science Advances* — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adu9368
- Multi-agent paradigm piece (arXiv:2506.01839) — https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01839v2

### Scope overrides
- Time window: 2023–2026, emphasis 2024–2026.
- Publication types: peer-reviewed + top arXiv.

### Exclusions
- Agent-system engineering papers without social-science framing.
- Business-process agent deployments.

### Additional constraints
- Include at least one source that critiques the validity / external-validity of LLM-agent simulations for social-science claims.
