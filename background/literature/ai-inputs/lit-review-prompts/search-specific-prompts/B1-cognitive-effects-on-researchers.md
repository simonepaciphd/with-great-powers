## Prompt context

### Paper context
- **Title:** With Great Powers: A Practical Guide to Agentic AI for Social Science Research
- **Thesis:** Effective agentic-AI adoption in social-science research rests on two principles — researcher control and radical transparency.

### Strand
- **Name:** cognitive-effects-on-researchers *(skill atrophy, cognitive offloading, expertise erosion under AI-assisted work — applied to researchers or professionals where direct researcher studies are thin)*

### Research questions *(DRAFT — researcher to confirm / edit)*
1. What empirical evidence (2020–2026) documents skill atrophy, expertise erosion, or deskilling among knowledge workers using AI tools, and what moderators (task type, user expertise, tool affordances, duration of use) have been identified as increasing or dampening severity?
2. Are there measurement studies specifically on researchers, scientists, graduate students, or domain experts using LLMs or agentic tools over extended periods? If the direct literature is thin, which adjacent literatures (medical AI-assisted diagnosis, programming copilots, translation, navigation / GPS) transfer most cleanly?
3. What cognitive-offloading mechanisms have been theorized — extended cognition, cognitive miserliness, automation complacency, metacognitive offloading — and which have empirical support in the LLM era specifically?
4. What is known about the learning side: when and how does AI assistance accelerate or impede skill acquisition (as distinct from reinforcing or eroding existing skills)? Any work on how AI changes graduate training, PhD skill formation, or early-career methodological development?
