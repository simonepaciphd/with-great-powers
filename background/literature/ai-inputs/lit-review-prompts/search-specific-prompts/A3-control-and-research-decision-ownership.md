## Prompt context

### Paper context
- **Title:** With Great Powers: A Practical Guide to Agentic AI for Social Science Research
- **Thesis:** Effective agentic-AI adoption in social-science research rests on two principles — researcher control and radical transparency.

### Strand
- **Name:** control-and-research-decision-ownership *(who decides what in human-agent research collaboration; human-in-the-loop design; autonomy gradients; epistemic authority)*

### Research questions *(DRAFT — researcher to confirm / edit)*
1. What normative arguments have been made about which research decisions must remain human-owned — question formulation, theoretical framing, specification choice, interpretation, final phrasing — when AI is in the pipeline, and on what grounds (epistemic, ethical, accountability)?
2. What design patterns have been proposed for human-in-the-loop research workflows — decision checkpoints, approval gates, autonomy gradients, contestability mechanisms, audit logs? From which literatures (HCI, STS, applied AI, CS)?
3. What empirical evidence exists on quality or validity outcomes when specific research decisions (coding, specification choice, interpretation, draft-writing) are partly or fully delegated to LLMs or agents? Are there documented cases of quality gain, loss, or null effect?
4. How have science-and-technology-studies (STS) scholars theorized epistemic authority when research outputs are partly machine-produced? Does prior work on automation, expertise, and scientific objectivity transfer cleanly to the LLM setting, or does agentic AI require new conceptual vocabulary?
