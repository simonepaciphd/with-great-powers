# trust-in-ai-research — Literature landscape

## Top 3 most relevant sources
1. barrie2025replication — The best single source for your exact strand because it directly theorizes LM replication problems in political science and links them to concrete workflow practices.
2. abdurahman2025primer — The strongest peer-reviewed social-science methods piece on how to evaluate, document, and review LLM-assisted research for validity and replicability.
3. gordon2025datanomad — The clearest integrity-infrastructure contribution in the set because it moves from disclosure norms to a concrete verification tool for archived research data.

## Themes identified
- **Open versus proprietary models as a reproducibility question**: A recurring claim is that proprietary hosted models create special replication burdens because they are updated, deprecated, and only partially documented, whereas open-weight models preserve the possibility of exact reruns and external audit. Representative sources: spirling2023opensource, palmer2024explicit, barrie2025replication.
- **Drift, prompt sensitivity, and model choice as empirical threats**: Several recent studies move beyond abstract concern and show that outputs vary over time, across prompts, and across aligned versus unaligned models. The upshot is that “the model” is not a stable treatment unless researchers document versioning and perform sensitivity checks. Representative sources: chen2024behavior, barrie2025promptstability, carlson2026annotation, thomas2026jagged, lyman2025alignment.
- **Classical researcher-degrees-of-freedom concerns are being re-expressed in LLM workflows**: The pre-LLM literature on p-hacking, forking paths, and multiverse analysis provides the cleanest conceptual vocabulary for agentic specification search. The LLM-era contribution is to show that prompts, models, and orchestration decisions add new analytic branches that are easy to underreport. Representative sources: simmons2011falsepositive, gelman2013forking, steegen2016multiverse, barrie2025promptstability, carlson2026annotation.
- **Social-science methods papers are converging on documentation and validation rather than blanket rejection**: Recent methods work generally treats LLMs as potentially useful but insists on clearer validation targets, stronger documentation, and narrower causal or inferential claims. Representative sources: abdurahman2025primer, tornberg2024annotation, stuhler2025promptbooks, lin2025fallacies, lyman2025alignment.
- **Integrity and reporting infrastructure remains emergent**: Beyond Data-NoMAD, the literature is heavier on reporting checklists and editorial disclosure rules than on end-to-end technical infrastructure. PRISMA-trAIce and journal policies show movement toward standardized reporting, but not yet standardized reproducibility stacks for agentic research. Representative sources: gordon2025datanomad, holst2025prismatraice, aer2026aipolicy, apsr2026aitools.

## Tensions / debates
- **Open-weight control versus frontier-model capability**: Spirling/Palmer/Barrie push strongly toward open models for replicability and auditability, while Korinek gives the strongest mainstream steelman for continued adoption of frontier generative AI because of immediate research productivity gains and broad usefulness. Sources: spirling2023opensource, palmer2024explicit, barrie2025replication, korinek2023generative.
- **Simulation aid versus human substitute**: Some work treats LLMs as useful research instruments under validation and task constraints, while Lin argues that treating them as replacements for human subjects leads to recurrent interpretive errors. Sources: abdurahman2025primer, lyman2025alignment, lin2025fallacies.
- **Disclosure versus reproducibility**: Publisher and journal policies increasingly require authors to disclose AI use, but disclosure alone does not solve rerunnability, deprecation, or prompt-version traceability. Sources: aer2026aipolicy, apsr2026aitools, gordon2025datanomad, holst2025prismatraice.

## Gaps
The literature is still thinner than your research questions assume on four points. First, there are few social-science-native studies that directly estimate reproducibility failure rates for published LLM-assisted empirical work across time, researchers, and prompt variants; the strongest direct evidence is still scattered across adjacent fields and targeted methods studies. Second, publisher and journal standards focus more on disclosure and authorship than on full workflow reproducibility, prompt archiving, or cryptographic attestation. Third, association-level standards specific to APSA, SPSA, or ASA were not easy to verify in this session; most concrete guidance came from journals and publishers instead. Fourth, integrity infrastructure beyond Data-NoMAD remains fragmented: there are proposals and checklists, but no settled social-science standard for versioned prompt archives, containerized LM reruns, or attested agent traces.
